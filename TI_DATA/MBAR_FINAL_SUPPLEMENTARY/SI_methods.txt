SUPPLEMENTARY METHODS -- MBAR Free-Energy Convergence Analysis
================================================================

Alchemical setup. Binding free energies for eight alchemical legs
(three 6-4 photoproduct lesion/partner-strand systems in native and
docked poses) were computed via a 12-window alchemical pathway
(lambda = 0.0092, 0.0479, 0.1150, 0.2063, 0.3161, 0.4374, 0.5626,
0.6839, 0.7937, 0.8850, 0.9521, 0.9908), each window simulated for
10 ns in AMBER. The first 20% (2 ns) of each window was discarded as
equilibration, leaving 8000 production frames per window (1 ps output
interval).

Free-energy estimator. Free-energy differences were estimated with
the multistate Bennett acceptance ratio (MBAR; Shirts & Chodera,
J. Chem. Phys. 2008) as implemented in alchemlyb 2.5.0 (wrapping
pymbar 4.2.0). alchemlyb's MBAR estimator requires its u_nk input to
already be the reduced potential (dimensionless, U/kBT); the raw
AMBER-reported per-frame energies (kcal/mol; confirmed to match the
independently reported EPtot for the corresponding simulation step)
were divided by kBT = 0.596161 kcal/mol (T = 300 K) prior to fitting,
and the resulting dimensionless free-energy differences were
multiplied by kBT for reporting in kcal/mol.

Treatment of overflowed energy evaluations. For lambda windows near
the fully-coupled end evaluated at the far decoupled end-state, a
minority-to-majority fraction of frames produce a reduced potential
that overflows AMBER's fixed-width output field (printed as a run of
asterisks). These values were substituted with a large finite
sentinel (1e15 kcal/mol) rather than discarding the frame, since an
overflowed reduced potential is by construction associated with
negligible Boltzmann weight at that state regardless of its exact
(unprintable) magnitude; this was confirmed not to bias results by an
explicit sentinel-magnitude substitution test (1e15 vs 1e6 kcal/mol
produced bit-identical fitted free energies).

Treatment of correlated samples. Because MBAR's analytic uncertainty
formula assumes statistically independent samples, each window's
production trajectory was reduced to a set of effectively
uncorrelated samples before the primary fit. For seven of the eight
systems this used pymbar's statistical_inefficiency and
subsample_correlated_data on each window's own diagonal (own-state)
energy series independently. For one system (docked_bhd2), this
per-window-independent approach produced an numerically ill-conditioned
full 12-state system (window 1's unusually long correlation time
reduced its independent sample count to a small fraction of its
neighbors' counts); a forensic audit (reported separately) traced this
to the resulting extreme imbalance in per-state sample counts, not to
the underlying simulation data, the energy units, the lambda-state
indexing, or the sentinel treatment (each explicitly tested and
excluded). For this system, a uniform stride was applied identically
to all 12 windows instead, which resolved the ill-conditioning; results
were verified consistent across stride 5, 10, 20 and 40 (see
Figure S4) and against two independent non-decorrelated
implementations already present in this project.

Forward/backward convergence analysis. For each system, the
production trajectory of every window was divided into ten equally
spaced cumulative fractions (10-100% of the post-equilibration,
decorrelated/strided samples). "Forward" estimates used the first
f% of each window's samples; "backward" estimates used the last f%.
Each fraction was an independent full 12-state MBAR fit. Consistency
between forward and backward estimates was assessed at the 90%
fraction (first-90% vs last-90%, sharing 80% of the underlying data)
as the relative difference |Forward90 - Backward90| / |Final estimate|,
expressed as a percentage; the 100% fraction was excluded from this
comparison because forward and backward are, by construction,
identical full-data fits at that point.

Effective sample size and autocorrelation. Per-window statistical
inefficiency g and effective sample size N_eff = N/g were computed
from each window's own diagonal energy series via pymbar's
statistical_inefficiency. A low N_eff was not, by itself, treated as
evidence of non-convergence; it was interpreted jointly with the
stability of the forward/backward free-energy estimate.

Overlap analysis. The MBAR overlap matrix (O[i,j], the estimated
probability of a sample from window i being observed at window j) was
computed via pymbar.MBAR.compute_overlap() on the full decorrelated
dataset for each system. Minimum, median, and maximum adjacent-window
(|i-j|=1) overlaps are reported; a single very low adjacent overlap
was not, by itself, treated as invalidating a system's convergence
where the aggregate forward/backward and stride-robustness diagnostics
were otherwise stable, consistent with established practice that MBAR
overlap requirements pertain to the eigenvalue-based connectivity of
the eleven-pair chain in aggregate rather than every individual pair
in isolation.

Uncertainty estimation and robustness. The analytic MBAR uncertainty
(asymptotic covariance of the free-energy estimator) was used as the
primary reported uncertainty. For docked_bhd2, this was additionally
cross-checked against a bootstrap estimate (200 resamples, using
alchemlyb's built-in bootstrap uncertainty option) and a five-block
time-series estimate (independent MBAR fits on five contiguous,
non-overlapping trajectory segments, block-to-block standard error of
the mean). The bootstrap and block estimates were larger than the
analytic estimate (by roughly an order of magnitude), consistent with
the analytic formula's assumption of ideal sampling conditions; all
three estimates were reported rather than the smallest being selected.
