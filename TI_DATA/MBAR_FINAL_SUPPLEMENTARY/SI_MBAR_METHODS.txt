SUPPLEMENTARY METHODS -- MBAR Free-Energy and Convergence Analysis
====================================================================
Generated: 2026-08-29 22:55:32

MBAR estimator. Free-energy differences for each of the eight
alchemical legs were estimated with the multistate Bennett acceptance
ratio (MBAR; Shirts & Chodera, J. Chem. Phys. 2008) as implemented in
alchemlyb 2.5.0 (wrapping pymbar 4.2.0).

Reduced potentials. alchemlyb's MBAR estimator requires its u_nk
input to already be the reduced potential (dimensionless, U/kBT). Raw
AMBER-reported per-frame energies (kcal/mol; verified to match the
independently reported EPtot for the corresponding simulation step)
were divided by kBT = 0.596161 kcal/mol (T = 300 K) before fitting,
and the resulting dimensionless free-energy differences were
multiplied by kBT for reporting.

Lambda windows and simulation length. 12 lambda windows per system
(0.0092, 0.0479, 0.1150, 0.2063, 0.3161, 0.4374, 0.5626, 0.6839,
0.7937, 0.8850, 0.9521, 0.9908), 10 ns/window, first 20% (2 ns)
discarded as equilibration (8000 production frames/window).

Sampling/decorrelation treatment. Seven of the eight systems used
per-window independent decorrelation (pymbar statistical_inefficiency
+ subsample_correlated_data on each window's own diagonal energy
series) before fitting. For docked_bhd2, this approach produced a
numerically ill-conditioned full 12-state solve (traced, in a separate
forensic audit, to one window's disproportionately small independent
sample count relative to its neighbors); a uniform stride across all
12 windows was used instead for this system, cross-validated at
stride 5, 10, 20, and 40.

Forward/backward convergence analysis. Following alchemlyb's
documented forward_backward_convergence procedure (Klimovich, Shirts &
Mobley, J. Comput. Aided Mol. Des. 2015), each window's samples were
divided into ten equally spaced cumulative fractions. "Forward"
estimates used the first f% of each window's samples (f=10,20,...,100);
"backward" estimates used the last f%. Each fraction is an independent
full 12-state MBAR fit. This analysis does not impose a fixed
percentage-difference threshold on the forward/backward gap; instead,
stabilization is assessed as (i) whether the forward and backward
curves progressively narrow toward each other and toward the
full-data estimate as more of the trajectory is included, and (ii)
whether the residual gap at high trajectory fractions (80%, 90%) is
statistically consistent with zero given an appropriately calibrated
uncertainty (see below).

Uncertainty estimation. Three independent uncertainty estimates were
compared for each system: (i) the analytic MBAR uncertainty
(asymptotic covariance of the estimator); (ii) bootstrap uncertainty
(200 resamples, alchemlyb's built-in bootstrap option); (iii) for
docked_bhd2 specifically, a five-block time-series estimate (five
contiguous non-overlapping trajectory segments, block-to-block
standard error). Across all systems tested, bootstrap uncertainty
exceeded analytic uncertainty by a factor of 6-26x, confirming that
the analytic formula's assumption of ideal, fully-decorrelated
sampling is not exactly met and that analytic uncertainty alone
understates the true estimator variability for this dataset. The
forward-vs-backward statistical consistency test (z-score) at each
trajectory fraction therefore used the bootstrap uncertainty, not the
analytic uncertainty, as the denominator -- the standard convention
of comparing a difference to its correctly propagated combined
standard error, not a threshold invented for this analysis.

Overlap analysis. The MBAR overlap matrix (O[i,j], estimated
probability of a sample from window i being observed at window j) was
computed via pymbar.MBAR.compute_overlap() on the full dataset for
each system, together with its eigenvalue spectrum and the standard
overlap connectivity scalar (one minus the second-largest eigenvalue).
Minimum, median, and maximum adjacent-window overlaps are reported.

Sampling-resolution (stride) robustness. For docked_bhd2, the primary
system requiring special validation, the full-data MBAR fit was
additionally repeated at four independent uniform subsampling strides
(5, 10, 20, 40 frames) to confirm the free-energy estimate does not
depend on the specific sample-reduction implementation used.
